## Overview

Last week, [Siniša Husnjak](https://www.linkedin.com/in/sinisa-husnjak/) and I attended SICK's internal Technology Days in Waldkirch, Germany, representing our Varaždin office. This multi-day technical event brought together engineers, developers, and project managers from across SICK's global network, including teams from Germany, the United States, and other European locations, to present innovations, share knowledge, and discuss the future of sensor-based automation and Industrial AI.

![Antonio Janach and Siniša Husnjak attending SICK Technology Days 2025](/projects/career/antonio-janach-sinisa-husnjak-technology-days-2025.jpg)

We both presented our work through poster presentations, which sparked valuable technical discussions with colleagues from different locations. The format encouraged direct feedback and uncovered collaboration opportunities across teams working on similar challenges in different markets.

## Key Takeaways

**Industrial AI and Sensor Intelligence**  
A central theme throughout the event was SICK's investment in Industrial AI for automation. Multiple presentations showcased how AI-driven sensor systems enable predictive quality assurance in manufacturing, achieving up to 99% precision in detecting potential defects before they occur. Seeing how machine learning integrates directly into sensor hardware deepened our understanding of edge computing architectures and real-time analytics at scale.

**Cloud Infrastructure and Data Processing**  
Interesting conversations emerged around preparing cloud infrastructure to handle industrial IoT workloads at scale. Teams discussed challenges in container orchestration for distributed sensor networks, implementing infrastructure as code for consistent deployments across environments, and setting up proper observability and monitoring systems. Understanding how infrastructure needs to support thousands of sensors streaming data highlighted the importance of automated provisioning, security hardening, and reliable CI/CD pipelines for rolling out updates without downtime.

## Personal Reflections

Presenting alongside colleagues from different SICK offices was a good reminder of why these internal events matter. Explaining our work to engineers outside our immediate team was a nice challenge that forced clarity in communication.
The informal conversations between sessions were honestly the best part. Discussing real technical challenges over coffee with people working on similar problems in different contexts beats sitting through endless slide decks any day.

## Conclusion

SICK Technology Days was a solid experience. Met some smart people, had good technical discussions, and picked up a few ideas worth exploring. These events work because they're more about genuine knowledge exchange than formal presentations.

Great insights gained and connections made!
